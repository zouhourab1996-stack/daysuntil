About Black Friday 2026

Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday, November 27, 2026. The day after US Thanksgiving and one of the biggest shopping events on Earth — doorbuster deals, long lines (increasingly online), and the unofficial kickoff of the Christmas shopping season.

Why Black Friday moves every year

Black Friday is not a fixed date — it is defined as the Friday immediately after the fourth Thursday of November, which is US Thanksgiving. Because Thanksgiving is tethered to a weekday rather than a day of the month, Black Friday can land anywhere from November 23 to November 29. In 2026, Thanksgiving is Thursday November 26, which makes Black Friday Friday November 27.

That movable date is why the same December advertising campaign can have a longer or shorter runway from one year to the next, and why the gap to Christmas shifts by up to six days.

How long is the runway to Christmas 2026?

Black Friday 2026 is 28 days before Christmas Day, which falls on a Friday. That is a shorter-than-usual sprint: when Black Friday lands on November 23, retailers get 32 days; when it lands on November 29, they get 26. Twenty-eight days sits in the middle, and it compresses the shipping window — the last reliable standard-delivery dates for Christmas arrive within a week of Cyber Monday.

It also lines up almost exactly with Advent, which begins on Sunday November 29, 2026 — two days after Black Friday. If you are buying for children, that is the date the countdown calendars open, not December 1.

When do Black Friday 2026 deals actually start?

The honest answer is that Black Friday stopped being a single day years ago. Most large retailers in the US, UK and EU now run 'early Black Friday' pricing through the first three weeks of November, with the deepest cuts reserved for Thanksgiving week and the Friday itself. In practice the pattern looks like this: teaser offers from around November 1, early-access sales for loyalty members in the second week, full-price-matching discounts across Thanksgiving weekend, and a second wave on Cyber Monday tilted towards electronics and software.

The useful discipline is to track the specific item rather than the day. Many 'doorbuster' headlines are genuine but limited to a handful of units per store; far more of the real discounting is spread evenly across the fortnight.

Black Friday vs Cyber Monday 2026

Black Friday is November 27 and Cyber Monday is November 30 — three days apart, both inside the same long weekend. Historically Black Friday was in-store doorbusters and Cyber Monday was the online answer, invented in 2005 when retailers noticed the Monday spike in e-commerce traffic. That distinction has largely collapsed: most Black Friday deals are now available online, and Cyber Monday has become the biggest online shopping day of the year in several markets.

Which is better depends on the category. Large appliances, furniture and in-store-only bundles tend to peak on Black Friday; laptops, software, subscriptions and small electronics tend to peak on Cyber Monday.

How to tell a real discount from a fake one

The most common trick of the weekend is not a lie, it is a comparison against the wrong number. A '60% off' label is usually measured against a list price the item has not sold at for months, and some retailers quietly raise that list price in the weeks before the sale to make the percentage look larger. The only honest reference is the price the same item has actually sold at over the preceding month.

Three checks catch most of it. First, track the item, not the banner — a price-history chart shows whether this is a genuine floor or a return to September's price. Second, watch unit pricing: multipacks and 'value' sizes are frequently more expensive per unit than the standard pack they sit next to. Third, be suspicious of model numbers — a television built for Black Friday often differs by one digit from the model reviewed in October, with a cheaper panel in the same shell.

Black Friday outside the United States

The event has travelled, mostly through the retailers that operate internationally rather than through any shared tradition. In the United Kingdom it arrived around 2010 via Amazon and Asda's Walmart ownership, and it is now the biggest shopping day of the British year — though it sits awkwardly there, because the UK has no Thanksgiving to anchor it to, which is why it drifted into a fortnight-long 'Black Friday week'.

It is now a fixture across Canada, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Brazil, Australia and much of Eastern Europe, usually as a purely commercial import with no holiday attached. In Germany the event is frequently rebranded as Black Week or Cyber Week, partly because the English name carries an association with a stock-market crash that local marketers would rather avoid.

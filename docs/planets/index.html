How these dates are worked out

Retrograde is not a planet moving backwards. It is an overtaking. Earth and the other planet are both orbiting the Sun at different speeds, and when Earth catches up and passes, the other planet appears to stall and reverse against the background stars for a few weeks before resuming its normal direction. It is the same effect as being passed on a motorway: the car you overtake seems to slide backwards even though it is still moving forward. These pages give the station dates — the moments the apparent motion turns — computed from Keplerian orbital elements rather than read off a table.

FAQ

Does a planet actually move backwards?

No. The apparent reversal is a line-of-sight effect caused by the Earth overtaking a slower orbiting planet.

How long does a retrograde last?

Mercury about three weeks, three or four times a year. The slower outer planets stay retrograde for four to five months at a time.

What is a shadow period?

The stretch either side of the retrograde when the planet is retracing ground it will cover again, or already did. Many astrologers treat it as part of the same window.

Are these dates computed or copied?

Computed from orbital elements, then checked against published ephemerides. They are the astronomical station dates, not a symbolic one.

How these dates are worked out

Some of these dates never move and some move every year, and the two need different kinds of confidence. Christmas is 25 December by definition; the only question is which weekday it lands on. Easter is the opposite: it falls on the first Sunday after the Paschal full moon, which is why it can land anywhere between 22 March and 25 April. Thanksgiving in the United States is the fourth Thursday of November, and in Canada the second Monday of October — so the same holiday sits six weeks apart depending on which side of the border you are counting down to.

FAQ

Why do some holiday dates change every year?

Because they are anchored to something other than the calendar. Easter follows the moon, Thanksgiving follows a weekday count, and Ramadan follows a sighting. Fixed-date holidays like Christmas only change their weekday.

Are these dates confirmed?

Fixed-date holidays are known years ahead. Moveable ones are computed from the rule that defines them. Where a date depends on a religious authority or a sighting, we say so on the page rather than presenting it as settled.

Do you count in my time zone?

Yes. Countdowns run in your local time zone, not UTC, so the number changes when your clock says it should.

What about public holidays that differ by country?

We list the date as it is observed in the country the holiday belongs to. If a country moves it to a Monday, that is noted on the event page.

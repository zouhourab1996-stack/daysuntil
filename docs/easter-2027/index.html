About Easter 2027

Easter 2027 falls on Sunday, March 28, 2027. The most important feast in Christianity, celebrating the resurrection — eggs, bunnies and spring festivals came along later. Easter is set by the first Sunday after the first full moon of spring, which is why it moves every year.

Why is Easter 2027 so early?

Easter 2027 falls on Sunday 28 March, which is towards the early end of the possible range. Easter can land anywhere between 22 March and 25 April, and 2027 sits seven weeks off the earliest possible date — noticeably earlier than 2026's Easter, which was 5 April.

The practical effect is that the whole spring season compresses. Lent begins in mid-February, the school Easter holidays in many countries fall in late March rather than April, and anything planned around the long weekend has to be arranged earlier than people expect.

How the date of Easter is actually worked out

In the Western church, Easter is the first Sunday after the Paschal full moon — the Paschal full moon being the first ecclesiastical full moon to fall on or after 21 March. That single rule produces the whole 22 March to 25 April range, and it is why the date drifts so much from year to year.

The word ecclesiastical matters. The Paschal full moon is a date calculated from a fixed table, not the astronomical full moon you would see in the sky, and the two can differ by a day or two. This is also why the calculation is reproducible centuries in advance: it is arithmetic, not observation.

When is Orthodox Easter 2027?

Orthodox Easter 2027 is Sunday 2 May — five weeks after the Western date. The gap comes from the calendar: the Orthodox churches still date Easter using the Julian calendar for the computus, and the Julian calendar is currently thirteen days behind the Gregorian one.

The two dates coincide in some years and fall a week, four weeks or five weeks apart in others. In 2027 they are close to as far apart as they get, which is worth knowing if you have family or colleagues celebrating on a different Sunday.

Is Easter a public holiday?

Good Friday and Easter Monday are public holidays in the United Kingdom, Ireland, most of Europe, Australia, New Zealand and Canada. In the United States, Good Friday is a state holiday in a dozen states but not a federal one, and Easter Sunday is not a federal holiday at all — most businesses trade, which is a common source of confusion for visitors.

Because Good Friday and Easter Monday sit either side of the weekend, most countries that observe both get a four-day break. That makes the Easter weekend one of the busiest travel periods of the year in Europe, and accommodation near major cities and coastal areas books up early.

Why the date matters for planning

An early Easter changes the shape of the school year. In much of Europe and the UK, the Easter holidays are tied to the date of Easter itself, so a late-March Easter pulls the spring term break into March and pushes the summer term two weeks longer. Families booking travel need to check their local authority's term dates rather than assuming.

It also compresses the gap between Easter and the May bank holidays in countries that have them, and it moves Mothering Sunday — the fourth Sunday of Lent — to 7 March 2027, which is early enough that it often gets forgotten.

What people actually do

The religious observance is the core of it for those who keep it: the Easter Vigil on the Saturday night is the central service in Catholic, Orthodox and many Anglican traditions, and dawn services are common in Protestant ones. For everyone else, the day is mostly about food, family and chocolate.

The specifics vary more than most people expect: in the UK hot cross buns and simnel cake, in Italy the colomba, in Greece the red-dyed eggs and the midnight feast after the Resurrection service, in Poland the święconka basket blessed on Holy Saturday, and in much of northern Europe Easter eggs and birch twigs decorated with feathers.

About daysuntil.bond

daysuntil.bond is a library of live countdowns to the dates the world is actually waiting for — 267 of them at launch and growing: holidays and observances, eclipses, full and new moons, planetary stations, meteor showers, solstices and equinoxes, and the biggest confirmed dates in gaming, movies and sport.

How the dates are computed

Astronomical events — moon phases, solstices, equinoxes and every planetary station — are computed to the minute with our own Keplerian ephemeris (JPL/Standish approximate elements, 1800–2050, tropical zodiac), cross-validated against published ephemerides. Holiday dates that move (Easter, Mother's Day, Thanksgiving and friends) are computed algorithmically. Entertainment and sports dates are verified against official announcements.

Accuracy notes

Lunar-calendar observances (Ramadan, Eid, Diwali, Chinese New Year) are marked "expected" where moon sighting can shift the date by a day. Countdowns to date-only events run to midnight in your local time zone; timed astronomical events count to the exact UTC moment. A predicted observance date is not a guarantee of the date later adopted by a local religious authority.

Primary references and date checks

Readers can check important calculations against the organisations that publish the underlying data. We use the NASA/JPL Horizons system for solar-system ephemerides, NASA's eclipse resources for eclipse circumstances, and the U.S. Naval Observatory data service for astronomical and calendar references. For U.S. federal holiday dates, consult the Office of Personnel Management calendar. For announced entertainment releases, the publisher or studio announcement takes precedence over any secondary calendar.

Why the countdowns are per-event rather than one big clock

Search behaviour decides this. Someone wanting to know how long until Halloween does not want a widget they have to configure — they want the number, and they want it to be right for where they are. So every event gets its own page, its own date table and its own set of ways to count it: days, weeks, weekdays, sleeps and the exact date. The number is computed in your browser against your own clock, which is why it changes when your day does rather than when ours does.

What we get wrong, and how it gets fixed

Dates slip. A game moves six months, a studio shifts a release, a lunar observance lands a day either side of a prediction. Where a date depends on a sighting or an announcement we mark it rather than presenting it as settled, and when something moves we correct the page. If you spot a date that is wrong, write to us through the contact page — corrections are the one kind of email we answer fastest.

What this site is not

It is not an astrology or fortune-telling service. Some event pages describe cultural, historical or astrological traditions as context; those traditions are presented as beliefs, not scientific findings. Planetary station times are astronomical calculations that describe apparent motion, not predictions about personal events. There are no horoscopes, readings or paid interpretations.

How the calculators get their numbers

Every calculator on this site shows its arithmetic rather than just an answer, because the point of a tool is that you can argue with it. Taking the fee calculator as the example: Fiverr charges buyers a service fee of 5.5% of the order value, plus a flat $3.50 on orders under a stated threshold. The tool applies both, in that order, and shows the components separately so you can see which part of the total is the percentage and which is the flat charge.

Fee structures change, and marketplaces revise them without much notice. Where a number could change, the tool states the date it was last checked rather than presenting it as permanent. If you spot a figure that no longer matches what the platform charges, the contact page reaches us directly and we would rather hear about it.

Why there is no email gate

The tools do not ask for an email address, do not set a cookie to remember your inputs, and run entirely in your browser. A fee calculation is not worth your inbox. It also means we cannot tell you what you calculated, which is the trade we are happy to make.

What we are building next

The tools exist to answer one buying question each, which is why there are so few of them. A calculator that tried to answer everything would be a form with a spiritual skin on it. What gets built next follows the questions that come up most in the reviews: where a category has a cost people consistently misjudge, that is a calculator worth writing. If there is one you wish existed, the contact page reaches us directly.

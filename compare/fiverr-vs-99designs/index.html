Fiverr vs 99designs in 2026: The $25 Gig vs the $299 Contest

The same logo brief can cost $25 on Fiverr or $299–$1,299 as a 99designs contest — and both prices can be the right choice. The difference is not quality; it is how many concepts you need and how well you can brief. Here is the honest math, including the fees both platforms add at checkout.

The per-concept math nobody shows you

A $249–$299 Bronze contest typically draws around 30 submissions. That is ~$8–$10 per concept — genuinely good value if you need to explore directions. But note what the contest model means: 29 of those 30 designers are paid nothing for their time, a ~3% win rate. It is a lottery for the designers, which is why top designers increasingly skip contests and work direct.

On Fiverr, $25–$130 buys one focused designer's best attempt, with revisions. If you can paste three logos you like, the single-designer route wins on speed, price and iteration. If you genuinely do not know what you want yet, the contest buys you a mood board of real work.

When 99designs is actually worth it

Three cases: you are rebranding and need board/committee buy-off (30 options defuse taste arguments); your brief is genuinely open-ended (new brand, no visual history); or you want the full copyright and identity-pack guarantees that come built into every 99designs tier. Outside those, the price premium buys variety you may not need.

The fees both platforms hide in checkout

99designs' 5% client platform fee appears at checkout, not on the pricing page — add it to every package before comparing. Fiverr's 5.5% + $3.50 is smaller but real, and the fee calculator shows the exact total before you commit. Sellers on both platforms keep 80% — a $35 Fiverr gig nets the designer $28, which is why the $5 tier is a lottery and the $25–$130 tier is the professional band.

First time buying on Fiverr?

Buyers pay a 5.5% service fee plus $3.50 on orders under $200 — and a $200 order costs less than a $199 one (exact math here). Your money sits in escrow until you approve the delivery, and for anything over $500 you should split the work into milestones. The single best habit: order the seller's smallest package as a paid test before committing your real budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is 99designs more expensive than Fiverr?

Yes, significantly. A 99designs logo contest starts at $299–$299+ with a 5% platform fee at checkout, while verified Fiverr logo gigs run $10–$385 with a 5.5% buyer fee. You are paying for 30+ competing concepts rather than one designer's focused work.

Do you get copyright on Fiverr logos?

Yes — Fiverr's standard terms transfer full commercial rights on delivery, but check the gig for vector source files (AI/EPS/SVG), which some sellers keep behind higher tiers. 99designs includes full copyright transfer on every tier.

Why are 29 of 30 contest designers unpaid?

That is how the contest model works: only the winning design is paid out of the contest fee. It gives buyers cheap variety but is a ~3% win rate for participating designers — one reason many top designers have left contest platforms.

Which is faster, Fiverr or 99designs?

Fiverr: most logo gigs deliver in 1–5 days. A 99designs contest runs 7–10 days plus feedback rounds, and needs your active participation to get good results.

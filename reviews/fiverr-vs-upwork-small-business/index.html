Fiverr vs Upwork for Small Business (2026): Real Fees, Real Costs, and Which One Wins for Each Job

Short answer: use Fiverr when you can describe the finished thing in one sentence, and use Upwork when you need a person for weeks or months. The fees are closer than most articles claim. The real difference is how you hire, how long you get to check the work, and how much of your own time each one eats.

Our verdict: For most small businesses, Fiverr is the better first stop for one-off work under about $1,500 (logos, a landing page, a product video, a bug fix). Upwork is the better pick for ongoing roles like a virtual assistant, a bookkeeper or a part-time developer, and for any project where you want to interview people before you pay. Many small teams end up using both.

How each platform works when you're the one paying

Fiverr: you buy a product

Fiverr feels like an online store. A freelancer lists a service with a fixed price, a delivery time and usually three packages (Basic, Standard and Premium). You read the gig page, check the reviews, and click to order. You pay up front. Fiverr holds the money until you accept the delivery.

You can also message the seller first and ask for a custom offer. That is the smart move for anything over about $50, because it lets you confirm scope before you pay. Bigger orders can be split into milestones, and Fiverr now supports hourly contracts for longer work.

Upwork: you run a mini hiring process

Upwork feels like a job board. You write a job post, freelancers send proposals with their rate and a cover note, and you pick who to interview. You can also browse profiles and invite people directly, or buy a fixed service from Upwork's Project Catalog, which works a lot like a Fiverr gig.

Fixed-price jobs run on milestones. You fund each milestone before work starts, and the money sits in escrow until you approve it. Hourly jobs are billed every Monday for the hours logged the week before.

The simplest test: if you can write the finished result in one sentence ("a logo in three file formats", "fix the checkout button on mobile"), start on Fiverr. If you'd describe it as a role ("someone to run our inbox 10 hours a week"), start on Upwork.

The real fees in 2026 (and two things most comparisons get wrong)

Both platforms charge the buyer a fee on top of the freelancer's price. Both also charge the freelancer, but that part is built into the price you see, so we leave it out here.

Fiverr buyer fees

  • Service fee: 5.5% of every payment you make.
  • Small-order fee: $3.50 extra on any payment under $200.
  • Each payment is separate: extras, tips and milestones each carry their own fee.
  • Hourly contracts: a $5 order initiation fee, then 5.5% on the hours billed.

Upwork client fees

  • Basic plan (free): a 5% marketplace fee on every payment to a freelancer. Eligible US clients paying from a checking account pay 3%.
  • Contract initiation fee: a one-time $0.99 to $14.99 per new contract, charged with the first payment.
  • Business Plus plan: 10% (8% for eligible US bank payers), with an initiation fee only on contracts of $100 or less (up to $4.99).

Two traps we found while checking. First, several popular comparison pages still quote old Fiverr fees, such as "$2.50 under $75". Fiverr's own payments article and Payment Terms say $3.50 under $200. Second, Upwork's pages don't fully agree with each other. The pricing page says 5% on Basic, while one Upwork help article says "up to 7.99%", and Upwork's promo terms mention a "flat" 7.99% structure for some accounts. Check the fee shown in your own Upwork billing settings before you budget.

The takeaway surprised us a little. On the same price, the fee gap is rarely more than $15. Fees should not decide this for you. Three other things matter far more:

  1. The freelancer's price itself. The same logo can cost $60 or $600 on either site. Comparing three good options on one platform saves more than switching platforms ever will.
  2. Your time. Writing a job post and reading 20 proposals on Upwork can take two or three hours. Buying a well-reviewed Fiverr gig can take 20 minutes. If your time is worth $50 an hour, that is real money on a $150 job.

Protection for buyers: the 3-day vs 14-day difference

This is the biggest practical difference between the two platforms, and almost nobody mentions it.

On Fiverr, once a seller marks an order as delivered, you have 3 days to accept it or ask for changes. If you do nothing, Fiverr marks it complete and pays the seller. If you need more time, Fiverr lets you extend the review period by up to 5 days, and its help center says you can do that more than once. For hourly orders, you get 7 days to approve each weekly report when you use manual approval (the default), or 72 hours if you switch to automatic approval.

On Upwork, you get 14 days to review a fixed-price milestone. If you don't respond, the money is released to the freelancer. Hourly hours are billed each Monday, and you can dispute them until Friday of that week.

What this means for you:

  • If you're a busy owner who might not check a delivery for a week, Upwork's 14-day window is more forgiving.
  • On Fiverr, set a reminder the moment an order is delivered. Asking for a revision inside the 3 days keeps your options open.
  • On both sites, keep every message and file on the platform. A freelancer who asks you to pay by bank transfer, crypto or another app is asking you to give up your protection.

Note: neither platform promises quality. Fiverr's own hourly terms say support won't judge disputes about how good the work is. Your best protection is a clear written brief inside the platform, agreed before you pay.

Finding good people: how vetting works on each side

On Fiverr

Fiverr sellers earn levels (Level 1, Level 2, Top Rated) from order history, earnings and ratings. Those levels show track record, not tested skill. Fiverr Pro is different: Pro freelancers go through a hand-vetting process and charge more. For a small business, a Level 2 or Top Rated seller with recent reviews in your exact service is a sensible floor. Use Pro when the work is high-stakes, like a full rebrand.

On Upwork

Upwork verifies every freelancer's identity and location before you can hire them, according to its pricing page. You also see each freelancer's full work history, client feedback, and badges such as Top Rated. Upwork's AI hiring assistant, Uma, can invite good matches to your job and build a shortlist. The Business Plus plan adds Expert-Vetted talent, which Upwork describes as the top 1% of its freelancers, but it doubles the fee to 10%.

A three-step check that works on both

  1. Read the last ten reviews, not the star average. Look for jobs like yours and for comments about communication.
  2. Ask one specific question before you pay. For example: "What do you need from me to start, and what is not included?" A good freelancer answers clearly and asks you questions back.
  3. Start with a small paid test. One blog post, one week of VA hours, or one landing page section. A $50 test can save you from a $1,000 mistake.

Pros and cons for small businesses

Fiverr: what we like

  • Fixed price and delivery time before you pay
  • Fastest way to get a defined job started, often the same day
  • Portfolios and package details right on the gig page
  • Fiverr Pro offers hand-vetted freelancers for bigger jobs (our Fiverr Pro review)
  • Hourly contracts now exist for longer work

Fiverr: watch out for

  • The $3.50 small-order fee hurts very small orders
  • Only 3 days to check a delivery before it auto-completes
  • Most sellers aren't skill-tested; levels show history only
  • Hourly needs a custom offer and at least 8 estimated hours
  • Lots of "guaranteed results" gigs to avoid

Upwork: what we like

  • Built for hourly and ongoing work, with weekly billing
  • 14 days to review fixed-price milestones
  • Identity-verified freelancers and full work histories
  • You can interview several people before you commit
  • 3% fee for eligible US clients paying by bank account

Upwork: watch out for

  • Hiring takes time: posts, proposals and interviews
  • Contract fee of $0.99 to $14.99 on every new contract
  • Some help pages mention fees up to 7.99%, so check your account
  • Business Plus doubles the fee to 10%
  • Vague job posts attract weak, copy-paste proposals

A simple plan if you're hiring for the first time

  1. Write a five-line brief. Goal, deliverable, deadline, budget, and one example you like.
  2. Decide the job shape. One finished thing? Go to Fiverr. A role or a project that will change? Go to Upwork.
  3. Shortlist three people. On Fiverr, compare three gigs at Level 2 or above. On Upwork, invite five freelancers and interview two.
  4. Pay for a small test first. Keep it under $100 if you can.
  5. Check the work fast. Within 3 days on Fiverr, within 14 on Upwork. Ask for changes inside the window.

The verdict

The two platforms now overlap: Fiverr has hourly work and milestones, and Upwork sells ready-made services. Their core still differs, so choose by that.

Pick Fiverr when you know exactly what you want and want it soon. It is the faster, simpler buy for logos, videos, fixes and one-off builds, and fees are on par with Upwork once an order reaches $200.

Pick Upwork when you are hiring a person rather than buying a product: a VA, a bookkeeper, a part-time developer. The interview process, weekly hourly billing and 14-day review window suit long relationships better.

Whichever you choose, the money is won or lost on the brief, the seller you pick and how quickly you check the work, not on the platform fee.

Frequently asked questions

Is Fiverr or Upwork cheaper for a small business?

On the same freelancer price, the fees are close. Fiverr charges 5.5% plus $3.50 on payments under $200; Upwork's Basic plan charges 5% plus a one-time $0.99 to $14.99 contract fee. A $500 job costs $527.50 on Fiverr and $525.99 to $539.99 on Upwork. The freelancer's own price matters far more than the platform fee.

Is Fiverr or Upwork better for hiring a virtual assistant?

Upwork, for most small businesses. Hourly work is its core model, with weekly billing every Monday and the chance to dispute hours until Friday. Fiverr now offers hourly contracts too, but they need a custom offer, a $5 start fee and at least 8 hours of estimated work.

Which is better for a logo or a one-off design job?

Fiverr. You can compare portfolios and fixed package prices on the gig page and order the same day. Just remember to review the delivery within 3 days, or extend the review period, before the order completes on its own.

How long do I have to check work on Fiverr and Upwork?

Fiverr gives you 3 days after delivery before an order completes automatically, and you can extend the review by up to 5 days. Upwork gives you 14 days to review a fixed-price milestone before funds are released automatically.

Does Upwork charge a monthly subscription to clients?

No. The Basic plan is free to join; you pay a marketplace fee only when you pay a freelancer. Business Plus is also free to join but charges a 10% fee (8% for eligible US clients paying by bank account).

Can I use both Fiverr and Upwork?

Yes, and many small businesses do. A common split is Fiverr for defined one-off jobs like logos, videos and fixes, and Upwork for ongoing roles like a virtual assistant, bookkeeper or part-time developer.

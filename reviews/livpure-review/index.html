Liv Pure Review: TrustScore 5.2/10

The liver-first weight offer that dominated 2023–2024, and has faded fast since. We no longer recommend it.

Liv Pure built a huge install base on one idea: clean up liver function and fat-burning follows. The framing is fresher than 'thermogenics', and the two-year-plus track record means the operation is stable.

The verdict: Skip it. The weight-loss claims aren't backed by evidence, buyer reviews point to refund and billing problems, and demand has collapsed. If you want liver support, ask your doctor about a plain milk thistle product; if you want weight loss, a calorie-based plan beats any "liver detox" pill.

⚠️ Verified facts (checked October 8, 2026)

  • Demand has collapsed. Liv Pure's ClickBank gravity (a rough measure of how many affiliates are making sales) is about 27, down from a peak of about 799, with a conversion rate around 0.15% (CBSnooper).
  • Trustpilot: 2 out of 5 from 16 reviews, almost all 1-star. Complaints include refunds that didn't arrive, double charges from add-ons, shipping deducted from refunds, slow email-only support and no weight loss (Trustpilot, liv-pure.com). Small sample, but the pattern is consistent.
  • Not FDA-evaluated. Liv Pure is a dietary supplement. The FDA does not evaluate supplements for safety or effectiveness, so any ad that calls it "FDA approved" is wrong. We found no FDA warning letter or FTC action naming it.
  • No weight-loss evidence for the blend. Its liver-support ingredients (such as silymarin from milk thistle) have indirect evidence at best, and "dissolves fat overnight" is marketing, not science.
  • Refund route: orders go through ClickBank with a 60-day window; if the vendor doesn't respond, request the refund via clkbank.com.
  • Name confusion: "Livpure" is also an unrelated Indian water-purifier brand, so check you're reading about the supplement.
💊 Not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before taking any liver or weight-loss supplement, especially if you have liver disease or take prescription medication (milk thistle and similar ingredients can interact with medicines processed by the liver).

Research-based review: we have not bought or used Liv Pure. How we review.

What you actually get

The sales letter wraps a liver-support formula (milk thistle territory, plus a 'Mediterranean ritual' narrative) in weight-loss promises. Liver health genuinely matters for metabolism — that part isn't fantasy — but the leap from 'supports liver function' to 'dissolves fat overnight' (their words) is where marketing takes over from biology. The 60-day ClickBank window applies, but buyer reports of slow or missing refunds (see the verified facts above) mean you should keep your receipt and go to ClickBank directly if the vendor doesn't answer. At best this is a liver-support supplement with a weight-loss story attached.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

✔ Made for you if…

Honestly, few people. If the liver angle appeals to you, a single-ingredient milk thistle product your doctor approves is simpler and cheaper.

✘ Skip it if…

Anyone who hears 'overnight fat dissolve' and believes it, or who wants clinical evidence for the weight claims specifically.

Frequently asked questions

Is Liv Pure legit?

It's a real product sold through ClickBank with a 60-day refund window, but Trustpilot reviews (2/5) report refunds that didn't arrive and double charges, and the 'overnight fat dissolve' claim is marketing fiction. We don't recommend it.

What's in Liv Pure?

A liver-support blend centered on silymarin-type compounds with a metabolic support stack; the full label is on the official site.

How much does it cost?

Typically $49–69 per bottle depending on bundle size. See the official site for today's price.

Who should not take it?

Anyone with diagnosed liver disease, or on medication processed by the liver, should talk to a doctor first — supplements are not medical treatment.

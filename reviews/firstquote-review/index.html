What you actually get

FirstQuote.ai solves a pain every local service business owner knows: you're on a job site, a lead arrives on Yelp, Thumbtack or Google Local Services Ads, and the first business to reply wins the job. FirstQuote connects to those lead sources and auto-replies in under 10 seconds — in your own voice and tone — then qualifies the prospect (SMS and two-way calls) and pings you when a lead is ready to book. The dashboard is a plain inbox with conversation summaries. The business model is the interesting part: no subscription. You pay per qualified lead ($2.49 for the first 30 each week, $1.49 after), and unqualified or duplicate leads are not billed.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

✔ Made for you if…

Local service businesses (moving, plumbing, cleaning, landscaping…) buying leads on Yelp, Thumbtack or Google LSA who keep losing jobs because they can't answer fast enough while working.

✘ Skip it if…

Businesses without steady inbound lead volume (there's nothing to auto-answer), anyone needing a full CRM (this is a response layer, not a pipeline manager), and skeptics who want a decade-old vendor — this is a young product.

Frequently asked questions

How much does FirstQuote.ai cost?

There is no subscription: you pay $2.49 per qualified lead for the first 30 each week, then $1.49 per qualified lead. Billing is weekly, unqualified and duplicate leads are not charged, and a 7-day trial needs no card.

Which lead sources does FirstQuote.ai support?

It connects to Yelp, Thumbtack and Google Local Services Ads — the channels where local service businesses buy leads — and replies by SMS and two-way calls.

Does the AI really sound like my business?

You train it on your tone, services and pricing examples, so replies go out in your voice. It's AI — supervise it early on and correct it when it drifts.

Is pay-per-lead better than a monthly subscription?

If your lead volume is irregular, yes — quiet weeks cost nothing. If you run high constant volume, do the math: 100+ qualified leads a week at $1.49 adds up, though it's still small next to what one won job earns.

How to buy translation safely

1. The test paragraph. Send 100 words of your real text before ordering; a native speaker's reply reads differently from a machine-post-edited one. 2. Certificates for official use. If it's for immigration or legal purposes, ask whether the seller provides signed certifications accepted by your authority - many don't. 3. One language pair per order - a translator fluent in eight languages is a machine with a profile picture.

Red flags

  • Instant delivery promises on long texts - speed this high is machine translation.
  • Rates far below the pair's market - the $2/500-word 'native' is not native.
  • No sample or portfolio text in the target language.

When NOT to hire

If the text is internal or low-stakes, machine translation plus your own edit is free and honest. Official documents may need a certified agency, not a marketplace gig. But for websites, listings and content that must read native to actual natives - the sellers above are the correct tool.

FAQ

How much does translation cost on Fiverr?

Standard human translation runs $10-$50 per ~500 words depending on the language pair; specialized documents $50-$150; books are priced per project from $200. In our table, entry prices run $5 to $250.

How do I spot machine translation sold as human?

Send a 100-word test paragraph before ordering and have a native speaker read the reply. Also check the seller's own gig description in the target language - machine-polished text has a recognizable rhythm.

Do Fiverr translators provide certified translations?

Some do, most don't. For immigration, legal or official use, confirm in writing that the seller provides the certification format your authority accepts before ordering - or use a certified agency.

Which language pairs are cheapest on Fiverr?

High-supply pairs (English-Spanish, English-German, English-Portuguese) run $5-$20 per small job. Rare pairs (Japanese-English native, Persian) cost more per word and are worth it.

Should I translate my whole website?

Start with the two or three pages that actually convert (home, pricing, checkout flow) and measure, rather than translating everything at once. Our Amazon-SEO translation entry shows the keyword-localization tier for product listings.

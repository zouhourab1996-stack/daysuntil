What you actually get

Templates are designed once in a drag-and-drop editor and deployed to the whole company, synced with your directory so new hires get the right signature automatically. The marketing layer is the differentiator: banner campaigns inside signatures with scheduling, segmentation by team or entity, A/B testing, native Canva integration and click analytics — every routine email becomes an impression. Digital business cards with QR codes are included. A free plan covers up to 19 employees (one template, one campaign, a Signitic badge); the paid plan is €1 per user per month from 20 licenses with unlimited templates and campaigns; SSO and CRM-list targeting sit in a custom tier. Hosting is in France with a GDPR posture to match.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

✔ Made for you if…

IT and marketing at 20-to-500-person companies that want every signature on-brand and a cheap, always-on internal marketing channel.

✘ Skip it if…

Individuals (it requires a company email), teams under 20 who won't accept the free plan's badge, and organizations that need monthly billing or Exchange on-premises support.

Frequently asked questions

What is Signitic?

Email signature management: design once, deploy company-wide from Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, and run scheduled banner campaigns inside signatures with analytics and A/B testing.

How much does Signitic cost?

Free for up to 19 employees (one template, one campaign, a Signitic badge). The standard plan is €1 per user per month from a 20-license minimum, billed annually, with unlimited templates and campaigns. SSO and CRM targeting are custom-priced.

Is there a free plan or trial?

Yes — a free plan for up to 19 employees with no credit card, and a free trial of the paid features. Small teams can run indefinitely on the free tier if the badge is acceptable.

Signitic vs Exclaimer or Letsignit?

The incumbents charge roughly €3–5 per user per month for comparable signature management. Signitic undercuts them at €1 with the campaign features included — the trade-offs are a younger track record and fewer enterprise integrations.

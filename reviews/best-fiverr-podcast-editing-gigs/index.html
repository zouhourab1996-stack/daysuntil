The filler-word trap

The most common budget surprise in podcast editing is the add-on table: many gigs price the base edit cheap and charge extra for filler-word removal, music licensing or loudness mastering. The fix is boring and effective — before ordering, ask one question: is filler-word removal and -16 LUFS loudness included in the base price? One message saves a doubled bill.

Zoom and Riverside specialists

Remote-recording platforms export messy multitrack files (local recordings that drift out of sync). Not every audio editor handles them well — Muhammad Arslan's gig is built specifically around Zoom and Riverside imports, and it is the right specialization to look for if your show is interview-based.

What to send your editor

  • The raw file exactly as recorded — not a version you already trimmed.
  • Timestamps of anything to cut or keep ("keep the rant at 23:10").
  • Your intro/outro files and music, licensed.
  • Your loudness target — the podcast norm is -16 LUFS stereo.

FAQ

How much does podcast editing cost on Fiverr?

Verified October 2026 prices run $5–$50 per episode. Audio-only clean-up starts at $5–$10, full editing and mixing sits at $15–$20, and video podcast editing runs $35–$50. A weekly audio show budgets $60–$80/month at the standard tier.

Is filler-word removal included?

Often not — it is the most common gig extra in this category. Ask before ordering whether filler-word removal is included in the base price, and get the answer in writing on the order page.

What about video podcasts?

Expect roughly 2–3× audio pricing: verified video-editing gigs run $35–$50 per episode versus $5–$20 for audio-only. Editors like Sam (774 reviews at $35) and Morrix ($50) specialize in the video side, and several include social clips.

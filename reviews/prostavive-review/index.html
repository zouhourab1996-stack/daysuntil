What you actually get

What ships is a jar of powder you stir into water (not capsules — a detail the ads bury and at least one Trustpilot reviewer learned the hard way). The formula reads like a men's-health checklist: zinc, boron, nettle root, Panax ginseng, ashwagandha, tongkat ali, maca root and friends — botanicals with real individual study histories in men's health, though no trial exists for this specific blend. The pitch page promises a 180-day money-back window, longer than most in the niche; ClickBank's own 60-day enforcement is the part you can actually rely on. Manufacturing claims are the standard 'FDA-registered facility, GMP' — a statement about the factory, not an endorsement of the jar.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

✔ Made for you if…

Men 45+ dealing with mild, annoying nighttime urination who want a plant-and-mineral daily scoop, understand supplements are experiments, and will actually enforce a refund deadline.

✘ Skip it if…

Men with diagnosed prostate conditions or on alpha-blockers and blood-pressure medication without a doctor's sign-off, and anyone who believes a powder replaces medical care.

Pros & cons

What we liked

  • One of the highest-volume men's health offers on ClickBank — fulfillment, billing and refunds are routine, not adventures
  • A coherent, fully-named ingredient list rather than a proprietary mystery blend
  • Powder format mixes into water once a day — no capsule fatigue
  • The 180-day refund promise on the pitch page beats the niche standard (ClickBank enforces 60 days regardless)

What could be better

  • Independent feedback is genuinely split — 'no change after six months' reports sit right next to five-star 'nights fixed' reviews
  • Per-scoop doses aren't published on the sales page, so there's no way to compare against studied amounts
  • $69 for a single bottle is premium; the pricing only makes sense at 6-bottle depth
  • The marketing's urinary-miracle framing overreaches well beyond what supplement evidence supports

Frequently asked questions

Is ProstaVive legit or a scam?

It's a real, reliably-shipped product with a working refund channel through ClickBank. It is not a medicine, and the independent feedback is split — that's a quality verdict, not a fraud verdict.

How much does ProstaVive cost?

Around $69 for a single 30-day jar, with bundles at $59/bottle (3 bottles) and roughly $39–49/bottle (6 bottles). Check the official site — these offers rotate pricing constantly.

The ads imply weeks. Realistic supplement timelines run 4–8 weeks of daily use, and honest reviews report everything from 'no change at all' to 'one trip a night instead of four'.

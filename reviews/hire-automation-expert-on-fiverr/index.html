The monthly bill the gig price does not show

The seller builds once. The automation tool bills you every month, and each tool counts usage in a different way. This is the cost most buyers forget.

ToolEntry price (official page, Oct 10, 2026)What it counts
n8n CloudStarter €20 a month billed annually, 2,500 executions; Pro €50 a month, 10,000 executionsOne execution per full workflow run, however many steps it has
MakeFree: 1,000 credits; Core $12 a month for 10,000 credits; Pro $21One credit for most module actions, such as reading or writing a row
ZapierFree: 100 tasks; Professional from $19.99 a month; Team from $69One task per successful action step; the trigger does not count

Here is what that means for a real workflow. Say yours has one trigger and five action steps, and it runs 1,000 times a month:

  • n8n counts 1,000 executions. That fits inside the 2,500 on the Starter plan.
  • Make counts about 5,000 credits, one for each module action. That fits inside the 10,000 on Core.
  • Zapier counts about 5,000 tasks, because each successful action step is a task. You would need a higher task tier than the entry plan.

Filters, formatters and paths in Zapier do not use tasks, and error handlers in Make do not use credits, so a careful builder can lower these totals. n8n also offers a free self-hosted Community Edition, but then you pay for the server and someone has to keep it running. Fiverr's guide puts ongoing maintenance agreements at $50 to $500 a month and automation tool subscriptions at $20 to $500 a month.

Before you order, ask: "How many runs a day do you expect, and what will that cost me each month on my own account?" A good seller answers with a number.

Why two quotes for the same job can differ by five times

Picture this brief: "When someone fills my form, add them to a sheet and message me." One seller reads three steps that work once in a demo. Another asks what happens when the sheet is renamed, the token expires, the form sends the same entry twice or the app is down for an hour.

Both can be honest. They are pricing different products. Brief & Hire's September 2026 guide names the usual drivers: the number of steps and tools, the number of decisions, whether it must run unattended, how messy the data source is, AI steps, where it runs, and hand-over. The cheapest quote often leaves out the unattended part, and that is the part you need most.

How to vet an automation expert on Fiverr: 9-point checklist

1. Write the task as numbered steps

List the trigger, each app, each rule and the final result. Sellers can only price what you write down. If you cannot describe it in steps, pay for a small paid consultation first.

2. Pick the platform before the seller

Search for the tool you want, not just "automation". A seller who says "I can use anything" is a weaker signal than one who explains why n8n suits your volume and Make suits your budget.

3. Ask for a failure alert

Every run that fails should send you a message. Without it, the automation stops quietly and you find out when a customer complains.

4. Ask for protection against double runs

If the same trigger arrives twice, you should not get two invoices or two emails. Ask how the seller prevents it.

5. Keep every account and key in your name

Create the automation workspace and the app logins yourself, then invite the seller. Never share your main owner password. Change any shared key after delivery.

6. Check reviews that match your order size

A 5.0 rating from $25 orders says little about a $600 build. Look for reviews that mention similar tools and that describe what worked after delivery, not just fast replies.

7. Ask for a running-cost estimate in writing

Platform plan, server if any, and AI usage per month. Put the answer in the order chat so it is part of the record.

8. Put an acceptance test in the brief

Here is a line you can paste:

Final payment after three unattended scheduled runs, one forced failure that alerts me, and one duplicate-run test. All credentials stay on my accounts.

9. Test inside the auto-complete window

Fiverr completes an order automatically three days after delivery if you do not respond. Run your tests in that window, not next week.

Red flags in automation gigs

  • "I'll host it for you" with no export. If the workflow only exists on the seller's account, you cannot move it.
  • Asking for your owner login or card details. A collaborator invite or a scoped API key is enough.
  • A price far below the category range with no scope. A $10 gig may be a template, not your workflow.
  • No word about errors, logs or testing. That usually means a demo, not a working system.
  • Pressure to pay outside Fiverr. You lose payment protection and the Resolution Center.

FAQ

How much does it cost to hire an n8n, Make or Zapier expert on Fiverr?

Fiverr's 2026 cost guide puts basic automation projects at $40 to $200, intermediate multi-app builds at $200 to $800 and advanced systems at $800 to $3,000 or more. In 317 reviewed orders, 39% cost $100 to $400, which is where a first workflow usually lands.

Is n8n, Make or Zapier cheaper to run?

For the same workflow, n8n and Make are usually cheaper than Zapier because they bill differently. n8n counts one execution per full run, Make counts a credit per module action, and Zapier counts a task per successful action step. Check the live pricing pages before you decide.

Should the automation run on my account or the seller's?

Yours. Create the n8n, Make or Zapier account yourself, connect your own apps, and invite the seller as a collaborator. If the workflow lives on the seller's account, you are renting it and leaving can mean a rebuild.

What should an automation acceptance test include?

Three unattended scheduled runs, one forced failure that sends you an alert, and one duplicate-run test. Final payment should wait until all three pass on your own accounts.

How long does a Fiverr automation take?

Fiverr's guide puts simple two-app setups at about 1 to 5 days, medium multi-tool builds at 1 to 2 weeks and enterprise-style projects at 4 weeks or more. Agree the date in writing and split anything over two weeks into milestones.

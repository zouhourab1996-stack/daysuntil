What you actually get

Search import/export records by product (HS code), company or route and you get actual shipment histories — who bought what from whom, quantities, dates and often prices. For supplier vetting, buyer prospecting and market sizing it's the kind of primary data that replaces weeks of guesswork. The trade-offs: it's expensive for casual use, data lags by country (weeks to months), and the UI carries the density of its data. As a sourcing or export-sales instrument it earns its keep; as a curiosity subscription it won't.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

✔ Made for you if…

Import/export businesses, sourcing teams and B2B sales teams selling to traders who need real shipment data.

✘ Skip it if…

Casual researchers and one-off lookups — the subscription math belongs to people who'll use it weekly.

Pros & cons

What we liked

  • Actual shipment-level data — real buyers, suppliers, volumes, prices
  • 190+ countries with HS-code and company search
  • Buyer/supplier prospecting that beats any lead database for trade
  • Market-sizing and competitor-shipping analytics built in

What could be better

  • Priced for professional use — casual subscriptions don't pay off
  • Data freshness varies by country (customs data inherently lags)
  • Interface is dense; learning curve is real
  • Some records redact consignee names by country rules

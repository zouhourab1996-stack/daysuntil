What you actually get

One dissolvable tablet a day delivers five probiotic strains intended to seed the mouth's microbiome — an inversion of the usual 'kill all bacteria' dental logic that has real research interest behind it. The format is excellent (no routine change, tastes fine), the operation is large and reliable, and the 60-day guarantee applies. What's missing: clinical trials on this specific formulation, and honest framing about how slowly oral-microbiome shifts happen. Expect subtle results over weeks, not the overnight transformations the VSL implies.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

✔ Made for you if…

People with recurring gum sensitivity or bad-breath issues who want to try the probiotic side of dental care alongside normal hygiene.

✘ Skip it if…

Anyone with acute dental problems — probiotics are not dentistry — and those expecting whitening miracles.

Pros & cons

What we liked

  • Oral probiotics are a real, growing research category — one of the few supplement niches moving in the right direction
  • Dissolvable-candy format is the best compliance design we've seen in supplements
  • Large, stable operation; routine fulfillment and refunds
  • 60-day ClickBank guarantee

What could be better

  • No clinical trials on this specific five-strain blend
  • Results are slow and subtle — the 'overnight' marketing sets the wrong expectations
  • $69 for a month of probiotic candy stings next to gut-probiotic pricing
  • Sales page conflates oral microbiome science with miracle claims

Frequently asked questions

Can ProDentim replace brushing or dental visits?

No — nothing replaces mechanical cleaning and professional care. ProDentim is an addition to hygiene, not an alternative.

Do oral probiotics actually work?

The category has promising early research for gum health and breath; this specific blend has no published trials. Our score reflects honest uncertainty.

How much does it cost?

Typically $69 for one bottle, ~$177 for three. See the official site.

Is it safe?

Probiotic strains are generally well-tolerated. Immunocompromised patients should check with a doctor first.

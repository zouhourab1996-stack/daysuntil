What you actually get

A liquid blend of grape-seed, green tea and a roster of botanicals marketed for '20/20 hearing' support. The uncomfortable truth: nutritional support for hearing loss has very thin evidence, and the most effective interventions in this space (protecting ears from noise, treating underlying conditions) are free or medical. Cortexi's operation is established and the guarantee is real, but our quality score reflects an evidence base close to zero for the stated purpose.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

✔ Made for you if…

People with mild age-related hearing frustration, already seeing an audiologist, who want to try everything available.

✘ Skip it if…

Anyone with meaningful hearing loss — delay in real care is the actual risk here — and noise-induced loss (the solution is protection, not drops).

Frequently asked questions

Can Cortexi restore hearing?

No supplement can restore hearing loss, and this one makes no approved claim to — the marketing implies more than any evidence supports.

Is any supplement proven to help hearing?

Research on antioxidants and hearing is early-stage and unconvincing for marketed products. Protection from noise and medical evaluation of underlying causes are the interventions with evidence.

How much does Cortexi cost?

Typically $49–69 per bottle with bundle pricing on the official site.

Is it a scam?

It's a real, shipped product with a working refund channel — but a weak evidence base. We score it low on quality, not legitimacy.

How to buy audio that fits

1. Send references, not adjectives. "Like NPR's pod-riff but warmer" beats "professional and modern". 2. Confirm the license. Podcast/YouTube use is standard; broadcast and paid ads need the broader license. 3. Ask for stems - the separate instrument tracks let any editor remix lengths later.

Red flags

  • Stock audio resold as custom. Ask for a 5-second custom sketch first - real composers can, libraries can't.
  • License vagueness. If the gig doesn't say where your jingle can run, ask before ordering.
  • One sample in the portfolio - you're hearing their one good day.

When NOT to buy

If your show publishes twice and dies (most do), a $15 stock-licensed intro is the honest budget. Commission custom audio when the show is proven - the jingle outlives hundreds of episodes. And if you need background music, not identity, subscription libraries beat custom composition on price every time.

FAQ

How much does a podcast jingle cost on Fiverr?

Simple instrumental intros run $15-$25, sung jingles $25-$60, and full composed themes $100-$211. In our table, prices run $15 to $211.

Do I need the stems?

If you'll ever need a shorter version - and you will - yes. Stems (separate instrument tracks) let any editor create 5-second, 15-second and full-length cuts without recomposing.

What license do I need?

Podcast and YouTube use is included in most gigs. Broadcast radio, TV and paid ad placements usually need an extended license - confirm in writing before ordering.

How do I brief a composer?

Send two reference tracks and one sentence on the feeling. Reference audio beats every adjective you can write.

Custom jingle or stock music?

Stock for unproven shows and background beds; custom once the show is committed - a jingle is your audio logo, and it compounds recognition across every episode.

What you actually get

Three phases (foundational flow, transitional flow, mastery flow) of follow-along videos that progress from beginner to genuinely challenging, built around 'dynamic sequencing' — increasing challenge as your body adapts. It's digital, one-time priced, instantly delivered, and requires nothing but a mat and floor space. The marketing is middle-aged and breathless ('yoga for flat belly'), but the product underneath is a legitimately well-structured home program — and at $37–57 one-time it costs less than a single studio class pack. Watch the order form: some offers in the funnel rebill monthly (coaching add-ons); decline them and you own the program outright.

Who it's for — and who should skip it

✔ Made for you if…

Women who want a structured, progressive home practice with zero equipment and no gym exposure.

✘ Skip it if…

Advanced yoginis (you'll outgrow phase two), and anyone who won't actually press play — the product can't do the yoga for you.

Pros & cons

What we liked

  • A real progressive methodology — three phases that genuinely build on each other
  • One-time price for lifetime access; cheaper than two studio classes
  • Instant delivery, works on any device, no equipment
  • Long-running, stable operation with a real refund channel

What could be better

  • Marketing leans on 'flat belly' body-image angles the program doesn't need
  • Funnel includes rebilling coaching add-ons you must actively decline
  • Camera-friendly production over alignment detail — purists will wince
  • Beginner-to-intermediate ceiling; no advanced path

Frequently asked questions

Is Yoga Burn a subscription?

The core program is a one-time purchase with lifetime access. Some checkout add-ons rebill monthly — decline them unless you want them, and check your order summary before paying.

Do I need experience?

No — phase one assumes zero flexibility and zero experience; the sequencing is the product.

How much does it cost?

Typically $37–57 for the core program depending on the current offer. Check the official site.

Is it just for weight loss?

No — it's a general progressive practice; the fat-loss framing is marketing. Flexibility, strength and routine benefits are the realistic outcomes.
